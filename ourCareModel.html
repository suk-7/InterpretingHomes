Our care model

One team, three clinicians, one plan.

Integrated behavioral health is something the system has talked about for years and almost never actually delivers. We deliver it — because for the Deaf community, fragmented care isn't an inconvenience, it's a barrier.

A typical client journey

Meet "Maria"

58 years old Deaf, ASL-primary Recent hospitalization

Maria was recently discharged after a cardiac event. She's adjusting to new medications, processing anxiety about her heart, and dealing with grief over how the hospitalization unfolded — no consistent ASL access, decisions made without her, three different teams that didn't talk to each other.

At Interpreting Homes, she has one care plan. Three clinicians. They actually coordinate.

A note about hospice

Dying in your own language matters.

Deaf hospice is one of the deepest gaps in American end-of-life care. Patients are placed in programs without an ASL-fluent person on the team. Decisions are made through whichever family member happens to interpret. Grief is processed alone.

Our hospice service is built around the team you've already met: a hospice-experienced RN, a doctoral psychologist for the family's emotional work, and an ASL-fluent care navigator who is in the room — in person, in ASL — when it counts most.
