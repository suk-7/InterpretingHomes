About

Started by people who lived the gaps they're filling.

Interpreting Homes is a Shelton family practice. Three healthcare professionals — a psychologist, a nurse, and an ASL-fluent care specialist — who spent years watching Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing patients fall through cracks no one was assigned to catch.

We kept the name even though we're not interpreters, because the work is about interpretation in the larger sense: making healthcare systems make sense, in the language and culture of the people we serve.

This is a small specialty practice on purpose. We're not trying to be everyone's everything — we're trying to be the right thing for the people the system keeps missing.

Frequently asked

Questions we hear often.

Where is your psychologist located? ×
Jonathan is a licensed psychologist in Arizona who holds an Authority to Practice Interjurisdictional Telepsychology (APIT) under PSYPACT — the multi-state compact that allows psychologists to provide telepsychology to clients in other PSYPACT states, including Ohio. All of his work with Interpreting Homes is via secure HIPAA-compliant telehealth. The same Ohio rules of clinical practice apply.
Do I need to know ASL to work with you? ×
No. We work in ASL, English, or a mix — whichever you prefer. We support Deaf, Hard-of-Hearing, late-deafened, and hearing family members alike. Some sessions are conducted in ASL directly; others use a mental-health-trained ASL interpreter alongside our psychologist.
Are you interpreters? ×
No. We are not RID or BEI-credentialed and do not provide independent interpreting services. We are clinicians who use ASL in our own care delivery and help you navigate the broader healthcare system. When ASL interpretation is needed inside a clinical session, we use credentialed mental-health interpreters.
Do you take insurance or Medicaid? ×
We're enrolled in Medicare for psychotherapy and home health. We're actively credentialing with Ohio Medicaid and the major managed-care plans (CareSource, Molina, Buckeye, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan), and Medicare covers our hospice service. Private pay and contracted partner arrangements are available today. We confirm your specific coverage in writing before any visit.
Can I get a psychological assessment for my SSDI or VA claim? ×
Yes. Jonathan has extensive experience with Social Security Disability Determination and VA Compensation & Pension exams. Assessments are conducted via telehealth, include standardized testing where appropriate, and produce a written report you can submit directly.
Do you provide hospice in [my county]? ×
Our in-home hospice service covers Greater Columbus and surrounding Franklin County. For families further afield, we can often arrange care coordination by phone or video and work alongside a local hospice provider. Reach out and we'll talk through what's possible for your situation.
What does "ASL-informed" mean? ×
It means the care itself is built around ASL and Deaf culture — visual communication, eye contact, plain-language English when written, no assumption that English is your first language, and respect for the community's history with healthcare.
Who's in the room during my therapy session? ×
For ASL-primary clients: Jonathan (via secure video) and a credentialed mental-health ASL interpreter. For English-primary clients (including most hard-of-hearing and late-deafened clients): just you and Jonathan, with captioning available. Robert provides support before and after sessions but not as the interpreter inside the clinical hour itself.
